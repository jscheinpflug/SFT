\title{Three-point boundary data from a patch:\\
derivative reconstruction by extension}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We rewrite the patch-to-boundary problem in derivative-first form. The primitive data are not boundary conditions at large radius, but exact derivatives along symmetry generators. In the scalar toy model these derivatives are the $SO(4,2)$ transport equations on the bulk and boundary orbits; in string field theory they are realized by the massless effective brackets of the extended system. From a center-patch seed, repeated symmetry derivatives determine the full infinite jet, and flat transport extends that jet to the full boundary leg. The boundary two-point kernel, its inverse amputation map, the transported quadratic source, and the boundary three-point function are all defined this way. In the scalar theory the resulting transported objects can be evaluated explicitly in embedding coordinates, recovering the standard bulk-to-boundary kernel and conformal three-point structure. The only local dynamical datum left after symmetry reconstruction is the overall coefficient, which we compute by the explicit scalar contact integral. We then state the exact bracket data that would realize the same reconstruction in the $\mathrm{AdS}_5\times S^5$ extended system.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

\subsection{Bulk action}

To avoid unnecessary combinatorics, we first take three scalar species $\phi_i$ with masses $m_i$ and cubic interaction
\begin{equation}
S[\phi_i]
=
\int_{\AdS}\! d^5X\,\sqrt{G}\,
\left[
\sum_{i=1}^3 \frac12\left(G^{ab}\bdy_a\phi_i\,\bdy_b\phi_i+m_i^2\phi_i^2\right)
+ g\,\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3
\right].
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
The equal-field interaction $\frac{g}{3!}\phi^3$ is recovered by setting $\phi_1=\phi_2=\phi_3$ at the end. The AdS/CFT relation is
\begin{equation}
m_i^2R^2=\Dims{i}(\Dims{i}-4).
\label{eq:massrel}
\end{equation}

We describe Euclidean $\AdS$ as the upper sheet of the hyperboloid
\begin{equation}
-(X^{-1})^2+X^aX_a=-R^2,\qquad a=0,\dots,4,
\label{eq:hyper}
\end{equation}
embedded in $\mathbb{R}^{1,5}$. The center patch uses the independent coordinates $X^a$ with
\begin{equation}
X^{-1}=\sqrt{R^2+X^2}
=
R+\frac{X^2}{2R}-\frac{(X^2)^2}{8R^3}+O(R^{-5}),
\qquad X^2\equiv X^aX_a.
\label{eq:xminus}
\end{equation}
This is the only local input from the patch we will use.

\subsection{Boundary source on the null cone}

The conformal boundary is the projective null cone
\begin{equation}
Y^M\in\mathbb{R}^{1,5},
\qquad
Y^2=0,
\qquad
Y\sim \alpha Y.
\end{equation}
A scalar primary of dimension $\Delta$ is represented by a homogeneous operator
\begin{equation}
\OO_\Delta(\alpha Y)=\alpha^{-\Delta}\OO_\Delta(Y).
\end{equation}
The source must therefore have homogeneity
\begin{equation}
J_\Delta(\alpha Y)=\alpha^{\Delta-4}J_\Delta(Y),
\label{eq:Jhom}
\end{equation}
so that the boundary coupling
\begin{equation}
S_{\rm src}[J]
=
\int D^4Y\,J_\Delta(Y)\OO_\Delta(Y)
\label{eq:src}
\end{equation}
is projectively well-defined.

Concretely, choose any affine section $Y(y)$ of the null cone such that
\begin{equation}
-2Y(y_1)\cdot Y(y_2)=|y_{12}|^2.
\label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
In that gauge,
\begin{equation}
\int D^4Y\,f(Y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^4}\! d^4y\,f(Y(y)),
\qquad
J_\Delta(Y(y))\equiv j_\Delta(y),
\qquad
\OO_\Delta(Y(y))\equiv O_\Delta(y),
\end{equation}
and \cref{eq:src} reduces to the standard $\int d^4y\,j_\Delta(y)O_\Delta(y)$.

\section{Derivative reconstruction from a patch}

\subsection{Scalar symmetry derivatives}

The primitive data are derivatives along the $SO(4,2)$ generators. Let $T_A$ be a basis of $\mathfrak{so}(4,2)$, let $L_A$ be the corresponding left-invariant derivatives on the AdS orbit, and let $\rho_A^{(\Delta)}$ be the induced first-order differential operators on homogeneous boundary functions of weight $\Delta$. In embedding coordinates this representation is
\begin{equation}
\rho_{MN}^{(\Delta)}=J_{MN}^{(Y)},
\qquad
\left(Y\cdot\bdy_Y+\Delta\right)f(Y)=0.
\label{eq:rhoY}
\end{equation}
The bulk-boundary leg is defined as the solution of the transport system
\begin{equation}
D_A^{(\Delta)}K_\Delta(X,Y)\equiv \left(L_A+\rho_A^{(\Delta)}\right)K_\Delta(X,Y)=0,
\label{eq:transportScalar}
\end{equation}
together with the scalar equation of motion
\begin{equation}
(\Box_{\AdS}-m^2)K_\Delta(X,Y)=0.
\label{eq:Keom}
\end{equation}
In embedding space \cref{eq:transportScalar} is exactly
\begin{equation}
\left(J^{(X)}_{MN}+J^{(Y)}_{MN}\right)K_\Delta(X,Y)=0.
\label{eq:Jcov}
\end{equation}

Choose the center point $X_\star=(R,0,\dots,0)$ and the formal center jet
\begin{equation}
K_\Delta(x,Y)
=
\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{1}{n!}
K_{a_1\cdots a_n}(Y)x^{a_1}\cdots x^{a_n}.
\label{eq:Kjet}
\end{equation}
The tangent directions at the center are generated by the coset generators, so every coefficient in \cref{eq:Kjet} is obtained by repeated symmetry derivatives. If $P_a$ are the tangent generators at the center, then
\begin{equation}
K_{a_1\cdots a_n}(Y)
=
(-1)^n
\rho_{P_{a_1}}^{(\Delta)}\cdots \rho_{P_{a_n}}^{(\Delta)}K_\Delta(X_\star,Y).
\label{eq:jetfromder}
\end{equation}
This is the exact all-orders statement available from the patch: the local data are the center seed and the exact derivative algebra.

Flatness of the $SO(4,2)$ action then extends the seed to the full orbit. If $\omega^A$ are the left-invariant one-forms on $SO(4,2)/SO(5,1)$, the transported leg is
\begin{equation}
K_\Delta(X,Y)
=
\mathrm{Pexp}\!\left[-\int_{X_\star}^{X}\omega^A \rho_A^{(\Delta)}\right]
K_\Delta(X_\star,Y).
\label{eq:Ktransport}
\end{equation}
This is the precise sense in which a patch calculation reaches the boundary: it computes the seed and all symmetry derivatives at the center, and extension transports that data to every bulk point.

\subsection{Scalar evaluation of the transported leg}

For scalars, diagonal $SO(4,2)$ invariance leaves only one invariant, $-2X\cdot Y$. The transported solution must therefore have the form
\begin{equation}
K_\Delta(X,Y)=c_\Delta\,(-2X\cdot Y)^{-\Delta}.
\label{eq:Kansatz}
\end{equation}
This is not guessed from a global boundary-value problem. It is the unique scalar function whose full symmetry derivatives agree with the transported center jet.

The ambient verification is immediate. Since $Y^2=0$,
\begin{equation}
\bdy_M\bdy^M(-2X\cdot Y)^{-\Delta}=0.
\end{equation}
For a function homogeneous of degree $s$ in $X$ one has
\begin{equation}
\bdy_M\bdy^M f
=
\Box_{\AdS}f+\frac{s(s+4)}{R^2}f
\qquad\text{on }X^2=-R^2.
\end{equation}
With $s=-\Delta$ this gives
\begin{equation}
\Box_{\AdS}K_\Delta
=
\frac{\Delta(\Delta-4)}{R^2}K_\Delta,
\end{equation}
which is \cref{eq:Keom} by \cref{eq:massrel}.

The normalization is fixed by requiring the source-to-bulk map
\begin{equation}
\phi_J^{[0]}(X)=\int D^4Y\,K_\Delta(X,Y)J_\Delta(Y)
\label{eq:phi0J}
\end{equation}
to reproduce the source in an affine section. There,
\begin{equation}
-2X(z,x)\cdot Y(y)=\frac{z^2+|x-y|^2}{z},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
K_\Delta(z,x;y)=c_\Delta\left(\frac{z}{z^2+|x-y|^2}\right)^\Delta.
\label{eq:Kpoincare}
\end{equation}
For $\Delta>2$ the choice
\begin{equation}
c_\Delta=\frac{\Gamma(\Delta)}{\pi^2\Gamma(\Delta-2)}
\label{eq:cDelta}
\end{equation}
ensures $\phi_J^{[0]}(z,x)\to j_\Delta(x)$.

Returning to the center patch and substituting \cref{eq:xminus} into \cref{eq:Kansatz} gives the explicit Taylor series generated by repeated symmetry derivatives:
\begin{equation}
K_\Delta(X,Y)
=
\frac{c_\Delta}{(2RY^{-1})^\Delta}
\left[
1+\frac{\Delta\,X\cdot y}{RY^{-1}}
-\frac{\Delta\,X^2}{2R^2}
+\frac{\Delta(\Delta+1)}{2}\frac{(X\cdot y)^2}{R^2(Y^{-1})^2}
+\cdots
\right].
\label{eq:Kpatch}
\end{equation}

\section{Boundary pairings and exact amputation}

\subsection{Transported dual leg and two-point kernel}

The outgoing object is a dual transported state. Let $\Xi_\Delta(Y)$ obey the dual transport equation
\begin{equation}
D_A^{(\Delta),\vee}\Xi_\Delta(Y)
\equiv
L_A \Xi_\Delta(Y)-\Xi_\Delta(Y)\rho_A^{(\Delta)}=0.
\label{eq:dualtransport}
\end{equation}
The boundary two-point kernel is the invariant pairing
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{K}_{2,\Delta}(Y_1,Y_2)
\equiv
\left\langle
\Xi_\Delta(Y_1),\Phi_\Delta(Y_2)
\right\rangle,
\label{eq:K2def}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_\Delta(Y_2)$ denotes the transported incoming leg. Because the pairing is preserved by transport,
\begin{equation}
\left(\rho_A^{(\Delta),\vee}+\rho_A^{(\Delta)}\right)\mathbb{K}_{2,\Delta}(Y_1,Y_2)=0.
\label{eq:K2inv}
\end{equation}
For scalars this fixes the form uniquely to
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{K}_{2,\Delta}(Y_1,Y_2)
=
\frac{B_\Delta}{(-2Y_1\cdot Y_2)^\Delta},
\qquad
B_\Delta=(2\Delta-4)c_\Delta.
\label{eq:GDelta}
\end{equation}
In the affine section this becomes
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{K}_{2,\Delta}(y_1,y_2)=\frac{B_\Delta}{|y_{12}|^{2\Delta}}.
\label{eq:Gaffine}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact amputation}

If one computes a true source derivative of the generating functional, no amputation is needed. Amputation appears when one works with the universal outgoing response
\begin{equation}
A_\Delta(y)=\int d^4y'\,\mathbb{K}_{2,\Delta}(y,y')j_\Delta(y').
\label{eq:response}
\end{equation}
The exact amputator is the inverse of \cref{eq:Gaffine}. In momentum space,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\mathbb{K}}_{2,\Delta}(p)
=
B_\Delta\,\pi^2\,2^{4-2\Delta}
\frac{\Gamma(2-\Delta)}{\Gamma(\Delta)}
|p|^{2\Delta-4},
\label{eq:Gmom}
\end{equation}
defined by analytic continuation, so
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{j}_\Delta(p)
=
\widetilde{\mathbb{K}}_{2,\Delta}(p)^{-1}\widetilde{A}_\Delta(p)
\label{eq:amputmom}
\end{equation}
is the precise momentum-space amputation.

Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
j_\Delta(y)
=
\int d^4y'\,\mathbb{K}_{2,\Delta}^{-1}(y,y')A_\Delta(y'),
\label{eq:amputpos}
\end{equation}
with inverse shadow kernel
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{K}_{2,\Delta}^{-1}(y,y')
=
\beta_\Delta\frac{1}{|y-y'|^{2(4-\Delta)}},
\qquad
\beta_\Delta
=
\left[
B_\Delta \pi^4
\frac{\Gamma(2-\Delta)\Gamma(\Delta-2)}
{\Gamma(\Delta)\Gamma(4-\Delta)}
\right]^{-1}.
\label{eq:shadowinv}
\end{equation}
The massless case is again defined by first keeping generic $\Delta$ and only then taking the limit $\Delta\to 4$.

\section{Transported quadratic source and the boundary three-point function}

\subsection{The source is a transported bracket}

For three fields, the local quadratic source is the outgoing component of the cubic bracket. In the scalar theory,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{J}^{\rm sc}_{12}(X;Y_1,Y_2)
=
g\,K_{\Dims1}(X,Y_1)K_{\Dims2}(X,Y_2).
\label{eq:sourceScalar}
\end{equation}
Because each incoming leg satisfies \cref{eq:transportScalar}, the quadratic source satisfies the diagonal transport equation
\begin{equation}
\left(
L_A+\rho_A^{(\Dims1)}+\rho_A^{(\Dims2)}
\right)\mathcal{J}^{\rm sc}_{12}=0.
\label{eq:sourceTransScalar}
\end{equation}
The outgoing boundary observable is the pairing with the dual leg of type $3$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{123}(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)
=
\left\langle
\Xi_{\Dims3}(Y_3),\mathcal{J}_{12}(Y_1,Y_2)
\right\rangle.
\label{eq:A123def}
\end{equation}
Combining \cref{eq:dualtransport,eq:sourceTransScalar}, one finds
\begin{equation}
\left(
\rho_A^{(\Dims1)}+\rho_A^{(\Dims2)}+\rho_A^{(\Dims3),\vee}
\right)\mathcal{A}_{123}=0.
\label{eq:A123inv}
\end{equation}
Thus symmetry transport fixes the full boundary-point dependence. For scalars the unique solution is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{123}(\Ypt1,\Ypt2,\Ypt3)
=
\frac{C_{123}}
{(-2\Ypt1\!\cdot\!\Ypt2)^{\delta_{12}}
 (-2\Ypt2\!\cdot\!\Ypt3)^{\delta_{23}}
 (-2\Ypt3\!\cdot\!\Ypt1)^{\delta_{31}}},
\label{eq:3pt}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\delta_{12}=\frac{\Dims1+\Dims2-\Dims3}{2},
\qquad
\delta_{23}=\frac{\Dims2+\Dims3-\Dims1}{2},
\qquad
\delta_{31}=\frac{\Dims3+\Dims1-\Dims2}{2}.
\label{eq:deltas}
\end{equation}
The only remaining dynamical datum is the local coefficient $C_{123}$.

\subsection{Scalar evaluation of the local coefficient}

In the scalar toy model the dual leg is represented by the same kernel, so
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{123}
=
g\int_{\AdS}\! d^5X\,\sqrt{G}\,
K_{\Dims1}(X,\Ypt1)K_{\Dims2}(X,\Ypt2)K_{\Dims3}(X,\Ypt3).
\label{eq:K123def}
\end{equation}
Transport has already fixed the $(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)$ dependence in \cref{eq:3pt}; the integral is only needed to determine $C_{123}$. Choosing an affine section and using \cref{eq:Kpoincare},
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{123}
=
g\,c_{\Dims1}c_{\Dims2}c_{\Dims3}
\int_0^\infty\!\frac{dz}{z^5}\int d^4x\,
\prod_{i=1}^3
\left(\frac{z}{z^2+|x-y_i|^2}\right)^{\Dims{i}}.
\label{eq:contactint}
\end{equation}
Introduce Schwinger parameters for each denominator. After the Gaussian $x$ integral and the $z$ integral one gets
\begin{align}
\mathcal{A}_{123}
=\;&
\frac{g\pi^2}{2}\,
\frac{c_{\Dims1}c_{\Dims2}c_{\Dims3}}
{\Gamma(\Dims1)\Gamma(\Dims2)\Gamma(\Dims3)}
\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\Dims1+\Dims2+\Dims3-4}{2}\right)
\notag\\
&\times
\int_0^\infty \prod_{i=1}^3 d\alpha_i\,
\alpha_i^{\Dims{i}-1}\,
s^{-\frac{\Dims1+\Dims2+\Dims3}{2}}
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{1}{s}\sum_{i<j}\alpha_i\alpha_j |y_{ij}|^2
\right],
\label{eq:schwinger}
\end{align}
where $s=\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3$. The Symanzik simplex integral then yields
\begin{equation}
C_{123}
=
\frac{g}{2\pi^4}\,
\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\Dims1+\Dims2+\Dims3-4}{2}\right)
\Gamma(\delta_{12})\Gamma(\delta_{23})\Gamma(\delta_{31})}
{\Gamma(\Dims1-2)\Gamma(\Dims2-2)\Gamma(\Dims3-2)}.
\label{eq:C123}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Massless equal-weight limit}

For $\Dims1=\Dims2=\Dims3=4$ one has
\begin{equation}
\delta_{12}=\delta_{23}=\delta_{31}=2,
\qquad
C_{444}=\frac{3g}{\pi^4}.
\label{eq:C444raw}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{444}(\Ypt1,\Ypt2,\Ypt3)
=
\frac{3g/\pi^4}
{(-2\Ypt1\!\cdot\!\Ypt2)^2(-2\Ypt2\!\cdot\!\Ypt3)^2(-2\Ypt3\!\cdot\!\Ypt1)^2}.
\label{eq:C444}
\end{equation}
If one computes instead the outgoing response variable propagated by the universal two-point kernel, the exact amputation is still \cref{eq:amputmom,eq:amputpos}.

\section{Extension turns all required data into string brackets}

\subsection{Massless effective symmetry derivatives}

The extended system already tells us how to convert derivatives into massless effective brackets. For the background $\psi$ and a massless effective gauge parameter $\lambda$, \cite{CGSY} gives
\begin{equation}
\delta_\lambda \psi
=
Q\lambda+\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n!}[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \lambda]'.
\label{eq:variationMasslessNote}
\end{equation}
Background super-isometries are generated by $\lambda_A$ satisfying
\begin{align}
Q\lambda_A+\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n!}[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \lambda_A]'&=0,
\label{eq:superiso1note}\\
\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{n!}[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \lambda_A\otimes \lambda_B]'
&=
f_{AB}{}^C\lambda_C
+Q\omega_{AB}
+\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n!}[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \omega_{AB}]'.
\label{eq:superiso2note}
\end{align}
These are the exact statements that derivatives along symmetry generators are realized by string brackets, up to the usual $L_\infty$ homotopy correction $\omega_{AB}$.

The effective covariant derivative on a physical fluctuation is therefore
\begin{equation}
D_A^\psi \Phi
\equiv
L_A\Phi
+\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{1}{n!}
[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \lambda_A\otimes \Phi]'
=0.
\label{eq:transportSFT}
\end{equation}
The dual outgoing object obeys
\begin{equation}
D_A^{\psi,\vee}\Xi
\equiv
L_A\Xi-\Xi\circ
\left(
\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{1}{n!}
[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \lambda_A\otimes \,\cdot\,]'
\right)
=0.
\label{eq:transportDualSFT}
\end{equation}
Flatness of transport follows from \cref{eq:superiso2note}. This is the direct string-field-theory analogue of the scalar transport system \cref{eq:transportScalar}.

\subsection{Every observable as an extended derivative problem}

Once derivatives are the primitive data, every ingredient in the boundary computation is an extension problem whose coefficients are string brackets:
\begin{itemize}
\item Incoming leg: choose a center seed $\Phi_{\star,i}$ in the physical cohomology and solve \cref{eq:transportSFT}. Repeated derivatives of the transported leg are generated recursively by repeated insertions of $\lambda_A$ brackets, exactly as in \cref{eq:jetfromder}.
\item Outgoing dual leg: choose a center dual seed $\Xi_{\star,i}$ and solve \cref{eq:transportDualSFT}. The boundary two-point pairing is the transported invariant pairing
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{K}^{\rm SFT}_{2,i}(Y_1,Y_2)
\equiv
\langle \Xi_i(Y_1),\Phi_i(Y_2)\rangle.
\label{eq:K2SFT}
\end{equation}
Its inverse is the exact outgoing amputator.
\item Quadratic source: define the outgoing component
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{J}_{12}^{(3)}(Y_1,Y_2)
\equiv
\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{1}{n!}
[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \Phi_1(Y_1)\otimes \Phi_2(Y_2)]'_3.
\label{eq:sourceSFT}
\end{equation}
Because $D_A^\psi$ is represented by brackets, the $L_\infty$ identities imply that $\mathcal{J}_{12}^{(3)}$ obeys the transported derivative equation obtained by differentiating the bracket and using \cref{eq:transportSFT}.
\item Boundary observable: pair the transported source with the outgoing dual,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{123}^{\rm SFT}
=
\left\langle
\Xi_3(Y_3),\mathcal{J}_{12}^{(3)}(Y_1,Y_2)
\right\rangle.
\label{eq:ampSFT}
\end{equation}
Its full boundary dependence is fixed by the transport equations. The only local dynamical data are the center seeds, the local quadratic bracket, and the inverse two-point pairing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exact extraction from the nonlinear extended-system solution}

Suppose the extended system is solved for the nonlinear fluctuation sourced by two incoming legs. Then the three-point coefficient is obtained directly from that solution by projection and amputation.

Let $\Phi_1(Y_1)$ and $\Phi_2(Y_2)$ be the transported incoming legs and let $\Phi_{12}(Y_1,Y_2)$ be the second-order fluctuation solving the shifted equation of motion
\begin{equation}
Q\Phi_{12}
+\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n!}[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \Phi_{12}]'
+\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{n!}[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \Phi_1(Y_1)\otimes \Phi_2(Y_2)]'
=0.
\label{eq:Phi12eq}
\end{equation}
This is the precise extended-system version of saying that the nonlinear field is sourced by the quadratic bracket of the two incoming legs.

Next choose a canonical outgoing dual $\Xi_3^{\rm can}(Y_3)$ solving \cref{eq:transportDualSFT}. Pairing it with the nonlinear solution gives the outgoing response with one external leg still attached,
\begin{equation}
A_{12\to 3}(Y_1,Y_2;Y_3)
\equiv
\left\langle
\Xi_3^{\rm can}(Y_3),\Phi_{12}(Y_1,Y_2)
\right\rangle.
\label{eq:responseSFT}
\end{equation}
The remaining leg is removed by the inverse transported two-point kernel
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{K}^{\rm SFT}_{2,3}(Y,Y')
\equiv
\left\langle
\Xi_3^{\rm can}(Y),\Phi_3(Y')
\right\rangle,
\label{eq:K2SFT3}
\end{equation}
namely by defining the amputated outgoing dual
\begin{equation}
\Xi_3^{\rm amp}(Y)
\equiv
\int D^4Y'\,
\big(\mathbb{K}^{\rm SFT}_{2,3}\big)^{-1}(Y,Y')\,
\Xi_3^{\rm can}(Y').
\label{eq:Xiamp}
\end{equation}
The amputated boundary observable is therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{123}^{\rm amp}(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)
\equiv
\left\langle
\Xi_3^{\rm amp}(Y_3),\Phi_{12}(Y_1,Y_2)
\right\rangle.
\label{eq:AampSFT}
\end{equation}

If the three external states admit a unique conformal tensor structure, symmetry transport fixes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{123}^{\rm amp}(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)
=
C_{123}\,
\frac{1}
{(-2Y_1\!\cdot\!Y_2)^{\delta_{12}}
 (-2Y_2\!\cdot\!Y_3)^{\delta_{23}}
 (-2Y_3\!\cdot\!Y_1)^{\delta_{31}}},
\label{eq:AampStruct}
\end{equation}
with the same $\delta_{ij}$ as in \cref{eq:deltas}. Hence the coefficient is extracted from the nonlinear solution by
\begin{equation}
C_{123}
=
\frac{
\left\langle
\Xi_3^{\rm amp}(Y_3),\Phi_{12}(Y_1,Y_2)
\right\rangle
}
{(-2Y_1\!\cdot\!Y_2)^{-\delta_{12}}
 (-2Y_2\!\cdot\!Y_3)^{-\delta_{23}}
 (-2Y_3\!\cdot\!Y_1)^{-\delta_{31}}},
\label{eq:Cextract}
\end{equation}
evaluated at any convenient triple $(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)$.

If only the $1/R$ expansion of the extended system is known, then each ingredient is expanded recursively:
\begin{equation}
\psi=\sum_{r\ge 0}R^{-r}\psi^{(r)},
\qquad
\Phi_i=\sum_{r\ge 0}R^{-r}\Phi_i^{(r)},
\qquad
\Xi_3^{\rm amp}=\sum_{r\ge 0}R^{-r}\Xi_3^{\rm amp,(r)},
\qquad
\Phi_{12}=\sum_{r\ge 0}R^{-r}\Phi_{12}^{(r)}.
\label{eq:Rexpansion}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
C_{123}(R)=\sum_{n\ge 0}R^{-n}C_{123}^{(n)},
\label{eq:Cexpansion}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
C_{123}^{(n)}
=
\text{coeff}_{R^{-n}}
\left[
\frac{
\sum_{a+b=n}
\left\langle
\Xi_3^{\rm amp,(a)}(Y_3),\Phi_{12}^{(b)}(Y_1,Y_2)
\right\rangle
}
{(-2Y_1\!\cdot\!Y_2)^{-\delta_{12}}
 (-2Y_2\!\cdot\!Y_3)^{-\delta_{23}}
 (-2Y_3\!\cdot\!Y_1)^{-\delta_{31}}}
\right].
\label{eq:Cnextract}
\end{equation}
Thus the truncated extended system determines the truncated three-point coefficient by three steps only: solve the transport equations for the external legs, solve the nonlinear equation \cref{eq:Phi12eq} for the sourced fluctuation, and then project with the amputated outgoing dual as in \cref{eq:Cextract}.

\subsection{Exact bracket data to compute}

Under the assumption that the full extended system realizes exact flat $\PSU$ transport, the boundary three-point problem is reduced to the following explicit bracket data:
\begin{itemize}
\item the background brackets $[\psi^{\otimes n}]'$,
\item the super-isometry brackets $[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \lambda_A]'$,
\item the symmetry-closure brackets $[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \lambda_A\otimes \lambda_B]'$,
\item the action of symmetry derivatives on physical seeds, namely $[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \lambda_A\otimes \Phi]'$,
\item the quadratic source brackets $[\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes \Phi_1\otimes \Phi_2]'$,
\item the cyclic two-point pairing on the transported boundary module and its inverse.
\end{itemize}
No further spacetime derivatives are independent inputs. Extension converts every higher derivative needed for the boundary observable into repeated insertions of the symmetry generators through these brackets.
